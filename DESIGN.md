# Design

Leuven as a tiny city you could hold in your hands: a warm, crisp, low-poly diorama at golden hour, with a calm, game-like HUD that stays out of the way.

## Principles

1. **The city is the interface.** The map fills the screen. HUD panels are small, few, and float at the edges. Every new control has to justify covering the city.
2. **Recognisable, not literal.** Real footprints and real heights make it unmistakably Leuven. Simplified shapes and a limited palette make it charming. Spend detail where people look: hero landmarks first.
3. **Crisp over smooth.**
   - Hard shadows with a softened rim, a 3-step toon ramp, dark ink outlines.
   - No blur, no glows, no soft gradients inside the scene.
   - Smoothness belongs to motion, not to surfaces.
4. **Fast is a feature.** It opens instantly and costs nothing when idle. A visual idea that breaks a budget in docs/RENDERING.md needs a cheaper version.
5. **Works by hand and by thumb.** Everything works with mouse, touch and keyboard, from a 320 px phone to a desktop.

## Scene

### Camera

- True isometric view (35.26° pitch).
- Rotation snaps to 90° steps. Held, it keeps turning and settles on the next quarter view ahead, never back.
- One rendering path: device resolution (capped at 2×), with multisampling only on standard-density screens, where edges would otherwise stair-step.

### Light

- Golden hour: a warm sun (`#ffe2b8`) from the south-west, long shadows.
- A cool sky fill (`#cfe3ff`) and a warm ground bounce.
- Shade keeps its colour: a shadow is a darker, slightly richer version of the surface's own hue, not the sky's blue-grey, so sand in shadow still reads as sand next to sunlit asphalt (`src/scene/lighting.ts`; a test holds every ground colour to it).

### Landmarks and squares

- **Hero landmarks get their real silhouette.** Low-poly halls, towers and spires fitted to the LiDAR: the Stadhuis' steep roof with dormers and six turrets, Sint-Pieterskerk's cross, flèche and squat west work, the Universiteitsbibliotheek's tower, Station Leuven's gabled middle and pavilions, Arenbergkasteel's corner towers, the Abdij van Park church tower. Enough to recognise at zoom 2–4, never sculpted detail.
- **Stepped gables say "Flemish town".** Nearly half the street-facing gables in the hero squares are stepped, a fifth elsewhere in the centre, none on the fringe. Narrow gables stay plain, since the steps would be smaller than a game pixel.
- **Ridges run the way the LiDAR saw them.** Most terraced houses have their ridge along the street, so a row reads as one continuous roof; a gable end faces the street only where the real one does.
- **Churches have towers and tall windows.** Every church whose tower the LiDAR saw gets a square tower and slate spire (or a slender spire on the ridge), and church walls get pointed lancets instead of the window grid.
- **Shops show what they sell.** Up close, every shop front has its trade's canvas awning over the door, a frituur, café, bar, bakery, pharmacy, ice cream parlour, bike shop, bookshop or bank a sign on a bracket down the street (a cone of fries, a cup, a pint, a loaf, an ice, a green cross), and a café or restaurant OSM says has a terrace a table and two chairs in its colour, where the pavement leaves room to walk past. Zoomed out, the painted canvas over the ground floor says it alone.
- **Squares show what happens on them.** Oude Markt gets rows of café parasols along its facades; the middle stays open.
- **Roads say what they're made of.** Asphalt, setts and paving stones come from OSM, kerbs and cycle tracks from the GRB. Setts get a grain once a stone spans a pixel or two and joints once it spans a few; from afar they're just a warmer, lighter grey than asphalt and a greyer one than the squares.
- **Paint is at least a pixel, or nothing.** Lane lines are never thinner than a pixel, and go only when zoomed far out; zebra bars appear once a bar spans about two pixels, and before that the crossing is a light grey patch.
- **Streets have furniture.** From zoom 2, the lamps, benches, bike racks, bus shelters and monuments OpenStreetMap knows, drawn a third larger than life so they stay a few pixels. Below zoom 2 they would be noise, so they hide. Jan Fabre's Totem on Ladeuzeplein (a 23 m needle with its jewel beetle) and the obelisk on Martelarenplein show at every zoom.
- **Racks are as full as Leuven parks them.** Packed by the station, well used on Ladeuzeplein and by the faculties, a third full in a quiet street, mostly empty behind a gate; a single loop by a door isn't drawn. Bikes stand in a run from wherever the first one parked, now and then a gap, never sprinkled along the rack, and a long rack outside the station holds ten at most. A low rail on two legs runs along each row, so a half-empty rack still reads as a rack.
- **A parked bike is a Leuven city bike.** A filled rear triangle that carries its colour from above, a diamond or a step-through frame, round wheels with spokes inside a ring of dark tyre, a black chain guard, cranks and pedals, a kickstand, a lamp on the fork, swept-back bars and a saddle (brown on a step-through, black on a diamond); on half of them a carrier with a red reflector, on one in ten a red crate on the front, on another one in ten a wicker basket on the bars. Every other one stands the other way round, each a few degrees off square with its front wheel turned, as bikes are left. Both tyres touch the street as it is drawn: the setts of a square, a pavement, a slope.
- **Monuments are a cast of toys, chosen by the data.** Each statue, bust, sculpture, column, memorial stone, fountain and pump is one of nine cartoon archetypes, never a likeness: a figure is legs, a torso, arms and a big head on a classic plinth, one arm raised or held out; a group is two or three of them side by side; abstract sculpture is stacked blocks, a gate or twin spires; a column has a stepped base and a pyramid tip; a fountain is a basin with a crown of spray, or with a figure pouring water over its head (Fonske). How tall each is, what it stands on, which way it faces and what it's made of come from the LiDAR, the GRB's plinths and OSM's tags, so Justus Lipsius stands high on his pedestal and a war memorial is a stone, not another bronze man.
- To add a landmark, fit parts in `src/scene/landmark-models.ts` against a LiDAR height plot of its footprint, list the footprint in `LANDMARK_FOOTPRINTS` (`src/data/area.ts`), rebake, then check it from all four rotations.

### Walking

- **A Leuven student dressed for a sunny day**, a little larger than life (1.3×, like the street furniture): a loose coral shirt with short sleeves and an open collar, cream shorts to the knee, bare forearms and calves, and white sneakers. The mood is a sunny 1980s seaside holiday, but the figure is our own, never a likeness of anyone. A few chunky blocks, never smooth or lifelike, and no more than reads from the chase camera: an octagonal head bevelled to the crown with hair at the back and a fringe, a neck, a boxy shirt broad at the shoulders that hangs straight over the seat of the shorts, a V of skin at the collar, and sneakers that spring up at the toe. Outlined in ink round every part but the eyes and the collar, and toon-shaded like the city, with knees, elbows and ankles so they can run and keep their feet flat.
- **Click a place, and you're there**, the way GTA drops its camera onto a character. A click or tap on the map (not a drag, a pinch or a long press) starts a walk on the street nearest it, within 30 m; W, Enter or **Walk** start one in the middle of the view. A marker in the walker's coral, ringed in ink, drops onto the spot; after a beat the camera dives at it from the map, tipping to look straight down on the way as GTA's sky camera drops, then swings in behind; the walker falls the last few metres with its arms up, lands on the marker squashed, with a puff of dust and a thud, and springs back to its height. Tap to standing takes 1.9 s, and the walker steers from the moment it lands (1.4 s). A spot with no street near says so: a greyed marker drops there, shakes its head and goes, with a low pair of notes and the hint. Leaving (Esc, or **Map**), the walker turns to the camera and waves while it rises the same way to the nearest of the map's four views. Either flight can be turned round: Esc while diving climbs back from there, and a click or W while climbing dives again from where the camera is.
- **A street-level chase camera**, the way third-person games do it: once landed, the camera rides a boom behind the walker. Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to bring it closer or further, smoothly; + and − step between clear distances, from over the shoulder to a high follow view that looks further down. It keeps the distance you chose rather than zooming in for every wall: a house or a tree may hide the walker for a moment (its silhouette shows through), and whatever the camera backs into dithers away rather than filling the screen. The far city fades into the warm haze of the map's horizon under a sky dome in the HUD's sky colours.
- **Walk or run.** Shift (or the **Run** toggle, or a thumb pushed to the stick's rim) runs. The figure turns on the spot or in an arc, never skids, and leans into curves; its head turns toward where the camera looks.
- **Ride any parked bike, leave it anywhere.** F (or the **Ride** pill, which shows within 1.6 m of a bike) gets on the nearest bike, walking up to it first if it is within 30 m. The bike keeps its rack look (frame, carrier, crate or basket, colour), is chunky beams under an ink outline like the figure, with what the parked one has and more: spoked wheels, mudguards, a chain, a lamp, a bell, and swings round to the open street as the rider gets on. It rides like a cartoon city bike: pedalling up to about 31 km/h in three seconds, 47 flat out, leaning into turns as far as the turn asks (never past 39°, never tipping over), coasting with the pedals level, braking firmly, and at a crawl walked round on the spot or backed up. The feet stay on the pedals and the hands on the grips; sprinting stands up and rocks with the cranks; stopping puts the left foot down. A wall met at a glance turns the bike along it; head on, it stops with a little bounce. F again (or **Get off**) brakes, puts a foot down and swings a leg over; the bike stays where it stopped, leaning on its kickstand with its bars turned. Up to six bikes stay where they were left for the visit; leave a seventh and the first goes back to its rack while nobody is looking.
- **Walkable** means anywhere a person could stroll: streets, squares, paths, lawns, car parks and open ground, and over every bridge. Never into buildings or their courtyards, tanks, the water, or through a bridge's parapet, a tree trunk, a bench, a lamp post or a parasol.
- **Hidden behind a building**, the figure shows as a checkerboard silhouette in the HUD's accent blue, which reads over brick, slate and pale walls alike.
- **A picked destination** gets ink-ringed pale dots along the way and a square target, Street View style.

### Plates on the facades

- **The city names itself, the way Leuven does.** Every house OSM gives a number carries it on a small enamel plate on its front, over its door where OSM maps the door, and every street a long plate on the corner buildings where it meets another, on both sides of it, so the name reads coming up the crossing street from either way (and along any 150 m without a corner), as Belgian streets do. Numbers that would touch move apart along the wall rather than overlap. They are part of the city: lit by the sun, in the shade with their wall, hidden behind the houses in front. No labels float over the view.
- **Enamel in the HUD's colours.** The street plate's `--sign` blue under a `--paper` rim, paper letters (7.6:1), and a thin ink edge so a plate reads on a pale plaster wall as well as on dark brick. Rounded corners and ink sides a few centimetres deep, so from an angle it is a plate, not a sticker. House numbers are bold (800) and fill their plate; street names take the HUD plate's letters (750), spaced a little wider than on screen so they stay apart from across the street, on a plate as long as the name.
- **Half again larger than life,** a little more than the walker and the street furniture: a number 30 cm tall, a street plate 45 cm tall, so they read from the chase camera across the street, on a phone too.
- **On the plaster, never on the glass.** They hang in the band the windows leave bare between the ground floor and the first: a number just over the doors and windows (over the awning of a shop), the way many Leuven numbers sit on the lintel, and a street plate above it, both under the eaves of a low house. The heights follow each house's own storeys, from its foot, as its windows do.
- **Flat on the wall.** Each plate takes the facing of the wall as it is drawn and stands a few centimetres off it, its inked sides reaching back into the wall, so none floats, sinks in or z-fights, on a slope or under a hipped roof whose walls stand on a simplified outline.
- **Only on foot, only nearby:** numbers within 40 m, street plates within 60 m. They don't show on the map or during the flight, and as part of the scene they don't move, so there is nothing to reduce for reduced motion and nothing for screen readers (the street sign names the street).

### Name boards on the shop fronts

- **Every shop says its name, the way a Leuven shopping street does.** On foot, each shop front carries its name in painted paper letters: the brand a chain's fascia shows, else the shop's own name, as OpenStreetMap has them. Like the plates, they are part of the city: lit by the sun, in the shade with their wall, hidden behind the houses in front.
- **On the awning, or over the window.** Where a front has an awning, the board is its valance: just in front of it, 1 m out from the wall and a little deeper than it, so the name reads from across the street and from down it. A pharmacy, a bank or a counter without an awning, or a front too near a corner for one, has a fascia board on the wall instead, in the band the facade paints over its shop window: above the glass, below the house number and the first floor's sills.
- **Its trade's colour, deepened.** The board is the darker of its kind's canvas and stripe (`FRONTS`), deepened by lightness alone until paper letters on it reach 4.5:1, so the hue stays (never mixed with ink, as with `--sign`): a café's green, a bar's oxblood, a frituur's red, a bakery's brown, a pharmacy's green. Paper letters (700) under a thin paper line inside an ink edge, the street plate's rim, with inked sides a few centimetres deep.
- **As wide as its front.** A board spans its awning, 10 cm short of either end, and is 0.4 m tall on an awning and 0.5 m on a fascia, the plates' half again larger than life. A long name makes it shorter, down to 0.26 and 0.32 m; past that it is cut with an ellipsis. Names longer than 22 characters are cut at a word where they can (`Brasserie De Vrijheid…`), and only Latin letters, figures and the punctuation names use are shown: a name in another script shows what it has in Latin letters, or no board.
- **Only on foot, only nearby:** boards within 50 m, with the plates and in the same draw call.

### Railways

- **Tracks read at every zoom, detail arrives as you zoom in.** Far out, a track is a light band (running lines) or a brown one (yards). As pixels get smaller than 0.9 m the catenary masts appear, under 0.5 m both rails as ink lines, and at the closest zoom the sleepers.
- **Masts only where they'd stand.** Every 60 m along electrified running lines, on the side with room, so a yard's inner tracks stay clear.
- **The station is the wave roof.** Leuven's platform roof is the most recognisable thing from above: four white vaults across the tracks, rising from the valleys to the crowns at their real LiDAR heights. Railway land is gravel, never lawn, except where OSM maps grass or scrub on it (the embankments).
- **Not walkable:** tracks, railway land and platforms. Platforms are raised to train-floor height and the walker stays at street level; roads under rail bridges stay walkable.
- Tunnels and covered tracks aren't drawn. Bridges get a paved deck over the water and a capped parapet on each open side; the ground follows the lie of the land, smoothed so the city's own steps (quays, cuttings, embankments) are gone, so roads stay at grade and pass underneath.

## HUD

The HUD is a frame round the city, the same in every mode: a corner panel at the top start (where you are, and the way back), the credits and Mute at the top end, the hint under them, the mode's one action in the middle of the bottom edge and a rail of view controls at the bottom end. The middle of the screen stays city.

### Tokens

Tokens are oklch, in `src/app/app.css`:

| Token                        | Use                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--ink`                      | Text, borders, hard shadows. Same family as the scene outlines.                                                                                |
| `--paper`, `--paper-press`   | Panel and control fill, a warm cream; pressed and hovered                                                                                      |
| `--accent`, `--accent-press` | KU Leuven-ish blue (the walker's x-ray): the mode's primary action and a toggle that's on                                                      |
| `--sign`                     | The street plate: the accent's hue a shade deeper. Deeper shades are set by lightness, never mixed with ink, which would turn the blue purple. |
| `--control`, `--control-big` | 44 px round controls; 60 px for Run                                                                                                            |
| `--bar`                      | 48 px: the action pills and the corner panel                                                                                                   |
| `--radius`, `--radius-inner` | 14 px panels; 10 px for what sits inside one, concentric                                                                                       |
| `--lift`, `--shadow-hard`    | The 3 px hard shadow every control stands on, and the 1 px one it sinks to when pressed                                                        |
| `--gutter`                   | Edge spacing, 12–20 px with the screen, plus the safe-area insets                                                                              |

### Surfaces

- 2 px ink border and a **hard 3 px drop shadow** with no blur: a nod to pixel UI that needs no transparency.
- **Pills** for actions, **circles** for view controls, a 14 px radius (`--radius`) for panels (the corner, the loading and error notice, hints). Things that belong together share one outline: zoom and turn are one capsule split by hairlines, the corner one panel. What sits inside a panel is 2 px in from its 2 px border, with 10 px corners (`--radius-inner`), so they stay concentric.
- **Enamel plates say where you are:** Leuven on the map, the street on foot, in the same place, the HUD's version of the plates on the facades. Nothing that looks like a plate is pressable.
- Inner radii are concentric (outer radius minus padding).

### Type

- System UI font, no web fonts, so nothing to download and no layout shift.
- Weights 600–800, sizes from 0.625 rem (credits) to 1.125 rem (the brand).
- Shortcut hints are monospace keycaps.

### Controls

- **One action per mode**, a pill in the middle of the bottom edge with an icon and a visible name, filled with the accent: Walk (and Ride or Get off, when riding comes). The shortcut shows as a keycap on devices that have hover.
- **The way back** is a bare button in the corner panel, before the plate, in every mode that has one: Map on foot (Esc). Top start is where apps put "back", and it keeps the bottom edge to one action.
- **View controls** are round, icon only, in a rail at the bottom end, in reach of a right thumb: a **compass** on the map whose needle points north and resets the view (R); then one capsule of **zoom in / zoom out** (+ / −; closer and further while walking), so pinch is never the only way to zoom, and **turn left / turn right** (Q / E). Each has an accessible name, and shows it with its key as a tip on hover and keyboard focus.
- **A mode's own controls join the rail under the capsule,** nearest the thumb: Run on foot; when riding, Bell, with Run as Sprint. The rail stands on the bottom edge, so what a mode adds pushes nothing sideways and the capsule moves only by the height of what joined.
- **Turning** acts as the button goes down. A tap is one step, a quarter turn on the map and an eighth on foot; held past 300 ms, the button or key keeps turning (on foot at the held Q/E rate, on the map steadily through the quarter views) until let go, when the map slows onto the next quarter view. Enter and Space press it once. Keys go by keydown and keyup, not the browser's auto-repeat. A sliding thumb or a long press doesn't end the hold or open a menu; letting go, the window losing focus or the mode changing does.
- Every control is at least 44 px. The action and the rail share the bottom row at every width (the action in the middle, the rail at the end), so a hint never pushes the controls about. On short screens (a phone on its side) the rail and its capsule lie down in a row, with Run where the compass was, so it never reaches up to Mute; tips then open above.
- **Map:** the Leuven plate in the corner, the rail, and **Walk** (W), which starts in the middle of the view; a click or tap on a street starts there. There is no separate step for picking a start (the command palette's "Walk here" starts in the middle too). Walking downloads on first use; if that takes over a quarter of a second, the hint says "Getting ready to walk…", and if it fails, "Walking couldn’t load. Check your connection and try again."
- **Walking:** the corner holds a bare **Map** button (Esc) and the street plate. The compass goes, and **Run** joins the rail under the capsule: a big round toggle, filled while on, labelled, under the right thumb.
- **By a bike,** the action slot shows **Ride** (F), primary. **Riding,** it says **Get off** (F), **Run** is labelled **Sprint**, and a round **Bell** (B) joins the rail above it. The first ride is coached in the hint line for a few seconds, once per device: "W pedals, S brakes, A D steer, Shift sprints. F gets off anywhere." (on touch, "Left thumb rides where it points; push to the rim to sprint.").
- **On touch,** a thumb that lands on the left of the screen grows a stick under it (ink ring, paper knob) and steers; anywhere else it looks around. Pushed to the rim, the stick runs.
- A spot that can't be walked to says so in the hint ("Can’t walk there. Pick a street or square.") until the next try, and on the map with a greyed marker that shakes its head where it was clicked.

### The street plate

- **Only while walking**, and only when the walker is on a named square or within 20 m of a named street. Names are OpenStreetMap's (`src/data/streets.ts`): a square wins over the streets round it, and at a corner the street already on the plate stays until another is 4 m closer, so it doesn't flicker.
- **An enamel plate inset in the corner panel,** beside the Map button, where the Leuven plate is on the map: accent-blue (`--sign`) under a paper rim, paper text, 14 px, centred, two balanced lines at most (up to 17 rem wide). It has no border or shadow of its own: the panel's ink outline and hard shadow hold both, so it reads as part of the HUD, and never sits in the middle of the view. The Map button keeps its size; a long name wraps instead.
- It slides in from the Map button's side, and each new name slides in after it (240–300 ms ease-out); with reduced motion, names only fade.
- It's a polite live region ("On Brusselsestraat"), so screen readers hear each new street once.

### Hints and coaching

- One or two lines, in a paper panel centred under the top corners, clear of both thumbs and the controls, always in the page (empty when there's nothing to say) so screen readers hear it change. It lets taps through to the map.
- **The first walk** is coached: keycaps (W A S D, Shift, drag, click) where there's a fine pointer; "Left thumb walks, right thumb looks" and a dashed ghost of the stick where the left thumb goes on touch. It stays until four seconds after the first step, and isn't shown again on that device (`localStorage`; if storage is blocked, it shows each walk). Everything it teaches is also on the controls' tips and in the map's description.

### States

| State    | Treatment                                                                                                                            |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Loading  | A small centred panel ("Building Leuven…") in one persistent `<output>` that also carries errors. Controls are disabled until ready. |
| Error    | A centred panel with a plain-language cause and a **Try again** button. Never a blank screen.                                        |
| Selected | Accent fill, with `aria-pressed`.                                                                                                    |

### Credits

- The data licences (ODbL for OpenStreetMap, GRB terms) require visible credit. OSM's attribution guidelines allow it to collapse only after it was shown.
- It sits in the top-end corner: "© OpenStreetMap" and "© Digitaal Vlaanderen" as two 10 px linked lines with a paper halo (each a 24 px target, reaching out above and below), beside (or, on narrow headers, under) a 20 px © button with a 44 px hit area.
- It collapses to just the © after 5 seconds, on the first pan, zoom or click, or when a walk starts; the © brings it back. The smoke test fails if the credit isn't visible at start.

### No labels on the map

Places aren't named over the city: floating tags covered the very streets they named and fought each other for room. The street plate names where you are while walking; the map has a text description for screen readers.

### Keyboard

- The map is one focusable widget (`role="application"`, focused on load without a ring).
- Arrow keys pan, `+`/`-` zoom, Q/E turn, R resets, W or Enter starts a walk in the middle of the view. M mutes or unmutes, in every mode.
- W A S D and Q E go by where they sit on the keyboard, on the map as on foot, so on AZERTY they are Z Q S D and A E. Hints, tips and the map's description name them as the keyboard in use types them, where the browser can say (QWERTY's letters otherwise). Keys named for what they do (M, R) go by letter.
- Riding: W pedals, S brakes and then backs up, A and D turn the bars (the keys drive like a vehicle, not relative to the screen), Shift sprints, B rings the bell and F gets off. The thumb stick steers by the screen: the bike rides where it points, as hard as it's pushed, sprints at the rim and brakes when pulled back past 135°. A click on a street rides there, slowing for the corners.
- Walking: W A S D or the arrow keys walk relative to the screen, Shift runs, holding Q/E turns the view, `+`/`-` bring the camera closer or further, Escape returns to the map. Clicking or tapping a street walks there along a route, shown as a quiet trail of small dots that shrink toward a thin ring. On the move the camera drifts back behind the walker a moment after the last look around.
- Shortcuts only act while focus is inside the app.

## Motion

- **Rotation:** 280 ms ease-out. Instant with `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Press feedback:** the control sinks into its hard shadow over 120 ms ease-out.
- **The street plate:** slides in from the side (240 ms), each new name after it (300 ms). With `prefers-reduced-motion` names only fade.
- **Hover:** only on devices with hover. Nothing else in the HUD animates; the compass needle turns with the view, frame by frame.
- **Walking and running:** a procedural cycle that blends from walk to run with speed, feet first. A foot that is down stays where it landed, flat and then rolling onto its ball, while the body moves on over it; the legs reach for the feet, and the hips ride as high as both legs allow, so a walk bobs a little. A run sinks onto each step and springs off into a moment with both feet in the air, pumps bent arms mostly behind the body and leans in. Setting off leans the body in with the hips ahead of the feet; stopping leans it back. Turning on the spot, it steps from foot to foot. The torso banks into turns, and the head follows the camera. Standing still, the walker breathes and glances about for four seconds, then rests.
- **The spawn** (above, under Walking): 0.12 s for the marker to land, a 1.25 s dive whose lens widens as it closes in so the first frame matches the isometric map, the walker's fall over its last 0.3 s, and 0.5 s of squash, dust and settling; leaving, a 0.12 s beat for the wave, then the same flight backwards. Every part runs on the clock, not the frame count.
- **The camera** It follows on springs: its look point trails the walker by a hair and leads it a little at a run, the lens widens slightly and the boom lengthens when running, and zoom eases in about a tenth of a second (docs/RENDERING.md, "How walking should feel"). With `prefers-reduced-motion` the map and the street cross-fade in a quarter of a second instead of flying, with no marker, fall or wave; the camera follows without easing and doesn't recentre, and the figure doesn't fidget. The walk cycle itself stays, since the user is moving the figure.
- **Future motion:** anything new (traffic, people, day-night) goes through Emil Kowalski's animation skills. It must also respect reduced motion.

## Sound

A tiny city sounds like a toy: felt and soft wood, never game-show beeps. Sounds are few, quiet, and made on the spot with Web Audio (`src/audio`), so there is nothing to download.

| Event                | Sound                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| HUD button           | A soft wooden knock: a short falling triangle tone, low-passed, under a 12 ms stick of noise                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Toggle (Run)         | Two felted notes a fifth apart, rising for on, falling for off                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Footsteps            | Each time a foot lands in the walk cycle: a heel thump, a triangle at ~150 Hz gliding down an octave, under a scuff of band-passed grit. Every step is a little different in pitch, level and timing, the right foot a touch lower; a run lands higher, harder and quicker |
| Walk starts and ends | The flight's air (a noise band sweeping up or down) and two soft notes, rising going down to the street and falling coming back up                                                                                                                                         |
| Landing              | The walker dropping in: a low thud, a triangle gliding from 120 to 60 Hz, under a puff of grit                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Can't walk there     | Two quiet low notes, falling                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Freewheel            | Coasting, a tiny bright click as each of six pawls passes, about 18 a second at a cruise, quieter slowly                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Bell (B)             | A "tring": two inharmonic sine partials struck twice, ringing on for half a second                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Kickstand            | A hard little clack over a low knock, getting on and off                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Hitting a wall       | A soft thud, deeper and louder the harder                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Walking to a spot    | A quiet tick on setting off, a lighter pair of notes on arriving                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

- **On by default, never before a gesture.** The audio context is made on the first tap, click or key after sound is on, as browsers require.
- **Mute** (M) is a round control in the very top-end corner, beside the credits: a setting, not a map control, so it stays out of the rail and out of thumb reach. It names itself in its tip, fills while muted so the corner stays calm while sound plays, and the choice is remembered.
- **Quiet:** a master level of 0.4 over voices of 0.03 to about 0.2, so even a running step peaks around a twelfth of full scale.
- **Silent while the tab is hidden,** and nothing plays per frame: each sound is scheduled when its event happens.
- Every voice rises over at least 3 ms and falls exponentially (no clicks), and passes through a filter: lowpass on tones for warmth, a band on noise. These follow Raphael Salaja's `create-sound` and `generating-sounds-with-ai` rules.
- No ambience loop: the city is quiet, and a loop would cost more than it gives.
- Surfaces don't change the step yet: the walk grid doesn't know cobbles from paving.
