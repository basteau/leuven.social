# Design

Leuven as a tiny city you could hold in your hands: a warm, crisp, low-poly diorama at golden hour, with nothing on screen but the city: every command is a keystroke away in one menu.

## Principles

1. **The city is the interface.** The map fills the screen, with no HUD: commands live in one searchable menu. What stays on screen is small, few, and has to justify covering the city ("On screen, and the menu").
2. **Recognisable, not literal.** Real footprints and real heights make it unmistakably Leuven. Simplified shapes and a limited palette make it charming. Spend detail where people look: hero landmarks first.
3. **Crisp over smooth.**
   - Hard shadows with a softened rim, a 3-step toon ramp, dark ink outlines.
   - No blur, no glows, no soft gradients inside the scene.
   - Smoothness belongs to motion, not to surfaces.
4. **Fast is a feature.** It opens instantly and costs nothing when idle. A visual idea that breaks a budget in docs/RENDERING.md needs a cheaper version.
5. **Works by hand and by thumb.** Everything works with mouse, touch and keyboard, from a 320 px phone to a desktop.

## Scene

### Camera

- **One camera, no modes.** An orthographic camera at the true isometric pitch (35.26°), from the first frame on: the street is the map zoomed in, not another view.
- **From the whole city down to a street.** Zoom 0.5 shows about 1,100 m of city top to bottom; zoom 40 a street about 14 m tall, three houses across. What is drawn is decided by how tall the view is, and nothing else (`src/scene/lod.ts`): street furniture under 280 m, the avatar's figure under 120 m, plates on the facades under 60 m.
- Rotation snaps to 90° steps. Held, it keeps turning and settles on the next quarter view ahead, never back.
- **Pinned to you until you drag the map.** The camera keeps the avatar in the middle without being mounted on it: the view's centre glides after it, a third of a second of its travel ahead, so the street ahead shows. Zooming while pinned (wheel, pinch, + −) zooms about the avatar, not the cursor. A drag past a tap's 8 px lets go, and the view is yours until Space (or Esc), W A S D, R or the arrow at the edge of the screen pins it again. A tap never moves the map. The pitch and the quarter view never change.
- **Close in, the street meets the horizon.** Under about 120 m of view, the far end of the street (the top of the screen) fades a little into the warm haze of the map's horizon, more the closer the zoom. On the map there is none.
- One rendering path: device resolution (capped at 2×), with multisampling only on standard-density screens, where edges would otherwise stair-step.

### Light

- **A day passes over the city.** The light follows a 24-hour clock (\`src/scene/daylight.ts\`): a cool blue night under a faint moon, a pink dawn from the east, a pale morning, a white midday from the south with short shadows, golden hour, a red dusk, night again. Between its keyframes the colours blend in Oklab, so a sunset goes through orange. The map opens at Leuven's own time of day; **Time of day** (T) steps to the next of morning, midday, golden hour, dusk and night, and **Let the day pass** (P) runs the clock at an hour every eight seconds, a day in about three minutes.
- **Night is lit from inside.** A share of the windows light up (most warm, a few cool), three shop fronts in four (a café's warm), and every street lamp glows and throws a pool of light on the street, drawn in the same pixel dither as everything else, at every zoom: from above, a city at night is its lamps. Nothing else changes: the same ink, the same toon ramp, only less sun and more lamp.
- Golden hour: a warm sun (\`#ffe2b8\`) from the south-west, long shadows. It is the keyframe the rest of the day is drawn round, and stays the palette's reference.
- A cool sky fill (`#cfe3ff`) and a warm ground bounce.
- Shade keeps its colour: a shadow is a darker, slightly richer version of the surface's own hue, not the sky's blue-grey, so sand in shadow still reads as sand next to sunlit asphalt (`src/scene/lighting.ts`; a test holds every ground colour to it).

### Landmarks and squares

- **Hero landmarks get their real silhouette.** Low-poly halls, towers and spires fitted to the LiDAR: the Stadhuis' steep roof with dormers and six turrets, Sint-Pieterskerk's cross, flèche and squat west work, the Universiteitsbibliotheek's tower, Station Leuven's gabled middle and pavilions, Arenbergkasteel's corner towers, the Abdij van Park church tower. Enough to recognise at zoom 2–4, never sculpted detail.
- **Stepped gables say "Flemish town".** Nearly half the street-facing gables in the hero squares are stepped, a fifth elsewhere in the centre, none on the fringe. Narrow gables stay plain, since the steps would be smaller than a game pixel.
- **Ridges run the way the LiDAR saw them.** Most terraced houses have their ridge along the street, so a row reads as one continuous roof; a gable end faces the street only where the real one does.
- **Churches have towers and tall windows.** Every church whose tower the LiDAR saw gets a square tower and slate spire (or a slender spire on the ridge), and church walls get pointed lancets instead of the window grid.
- **Shops show what they sell.** Up close, every shop front has its trade's canvas awning over the door, a frituur, café, bar, bakery, pharmacy, ice cream parlour, bike shop, bookshop or bank a sign on a bracket down the street (a cone of fries, a cup, a pint, a loaf, an ice, a green cross), and a café or restaurant OSM says has a terrace a table and two chairs in its colour, where the pavement leaves room to walk past. Zoomed out, the painted canvas over the ground floor says it alone.
- **No two houses alike.** Each house wears a style of its own, seeded by its id: plain, arched or tall windows (the tall ones behind a balcony rail of iron bars on the upper floors), a cornice under the eaves on most, a string course over the ground floor on some, a bluestone plinth on half, shutters on a pale house in five, a front door in its frames' paint under a fanlight in one bay in four, and brick that shows its courses up close. Shop fronts get a door at one end of the glass, with its wooden frame, handle bar and kick plate, and a mullion down a wide window. Roofs in the centre carry what a Leuven skyline has: a chimney stack on the ridge by the party wall (two on some houses), a dormer poking out of one slope of a third of them, a roof window in another fifth. The fringe keeps bare roofs.
- **The streets are alive.** Cars, buses, bikes and people move along the streets OpenStreetMap says they can (`src/data/traffic.ts`): cars and a few city buses on the roads, the buses keeping to the ring and the main roads; far more bikes than cars, as in Leuven, on the roads, the cycle tracks and the pedestrian streets, both ways down the one-way cycle streets; and people on the pavements, across the pedestrian streets and along the paths through the parks. Everyone is going somewhere: a destination a few streets off, by the shortest way for what they are (a car keeps to the through roads, a bike to the cycle tracks and the quiet streets, a walker to the paths and the pedestrian streets), never turning back at the next corner to get there, then another. Where they go across the street comes from the GRB's kerbs, measured on both sides of every way: cars in the carriageway, bikes by its kerb, and people on the pavement between the kerb and the facade, spread across it, on the other pavement when their side has none, and not along a main road that has none either side (the ring's carriageways); where a street has no kerb its ground is shared, a bike further right than a car and a walker by the facade. On a narrow street a car queues behind a cyclist rather than driving through it. Everyone follows the one ahead at a distance; slow for a corner and round it on a curve, swinging round rather than snapping; wait their turn at a junction someone else is crossing; signal before a turn and light their brakes when they slow. Near the view, people and cyclists are the avatar's own figure at its size, each in a shirt, shorts, skin and hair of their own, the cyclists on the rideable bike; cars and buses close by wear their detail (wheel arches, pillars, mirrors, bumpers, a grille, door seams, window pillars, two doors). Further off the same figure, squared off without its details or its outline, so a person is a person at every distance. Legs swing with the stride, on foot and on the pedals, the avatar's own gait; wheels and feet stand on the ground as drawn, pitched to the slope under them. By night headlamps, tail lamps, bike lamps and reflectors glow in a soft halo, and each car, bus and bike throws a pool of light on the street ahead that fades to its sides. Where a junction never clears, a driver waits six seconds and goes: no jams that last. How many are out follows how much street there is, a car every 270 m or so at a quiet hour, and cars keep to streets that go somewhere: not an abbey's lanes, a road for permits, deliveries or farm traffic, nor a street for those who live there unless that is where they are bound; a living street is driven at walking pace and never cut through. Most cars are bound beyond the view, so they pass through rather than circle, and nobody appears in sight: everyone comes in from the edge. Cars park in the colours a Belgian street does (greys, white, black, a blue, a red, a green); a bus is cream with a mustard band; cyclists and walkers wear shirts from the café guests' wardrobe. **Traffic** (C) empties the streets; with reduced motion they start empty.
- **Squares show what happens on them.** Oude Markt gets rows of café parasols along its facades, each over a table with its chairs and, at most of them, people sitting with a pils, a Trappist, a wine or a coffee; the middle stays open. Cafés, bars and restaurants put a clipped bay tree in a pot either side of the door, and houses wear framed windows with stone sills and, here and there, a box of flowers.
- **Roads say what they're made of.** Asphalt, setts and paving stones come from OSM, kerbs and cycle tracks from the GRB. Setts get a grain once a stone spans a pixel or two and joints once it spans a few; from afar they're just a warmer, lighter grey than asphalt and a greyer one than the squares.
- **Paint is at least a pixel, or nothing.** Lane lines are never thinner than a pixel, and go only when zoomed far out; zebra bars appear once a bar spans about two pixels, and before that the crossing is a light grey patch.
- **Streets have furniture.** From zoom 2, the lamps, benches, bike racks, bus shelters and monuments OpenStreetMap knows, drawn a third larger than life so they stay a few pixels. Below zoom 2 they would be noise, so they hide. Jan Fabre's Totem on Ladeuzeplein (a 23 m needle with its jewel beetle) and the obelisk on Martelarenplein show at every zoom.
- **Racks are as full as Leuven parks them.** Packed by the station, well used on Ladeuzeplein and by the faculties, a third full in a quiet street, mostly empty behind a gate; a single loop by a door isn't drawn. Bikes stand in a run from wherever the first one parked, now and then a gap, never sprinkled along the rack, and a long rack outside the station holds ten at most. A low rail on two legs runs along each row, so a half-empty rack still reads as a rack.
- **A parked bike is a Leuven city bike.** A filled rear triangle that carries its colour from above, a diamond or a step-through frame, round wheels with spokes inside a ring of dark tyre, a black chain guard, cranks and pedals, a kickstand, a lamp on the fork, swept-back bars and a saddle (brown on a step-through, black on a diamond); on half of them a carrier with a red reflector, on one in ten a red crate on the front, on another one in ten a wicker basket on the bars. Every other one stands the other way round, each a few degrees off square with its front wheel turned, as bikes are left. Both tyres touch the street as it is drawn: the setts of a square, a pavement, a slope.
- **Monuments are a cast of toys, chosen by the data.** Each statue, bust, sculpture, column, memorial stone, fountain and pump is one of nine cartoon archetypes, never a likeness: a figure is legs, a torso, arms and a big head on a classic plinth, one arm raised or held out; a group is two or three of them side by side; abstract sculpture is stacked blocks, a gate or twin spires; a column has a stepped base and a pyramid tip; a fountain is a basin with a crown of spray, or with a figure pouring water over its head (Fonske). How tall each is, what it stands on, which way it faces and what it's made of come from the LiDAR, the GRB's plinths and OSM's tags, so Justus Lipsius stands high on his pedestal and a war memorial is a stone, not another bronze man.
- To add a landmark, fit parts in `src/scene/landmark-models.ts` against a LiDAR height plot of its footprint, list the footprint in `LANDMARK_FOOTPRINTS` (`src/data/area.ts`), rebake, then check it from all four rotations.

### The avatar

- **A Leuven student dressed for a sunny day**, a little larger than life (1.3×, like the street furniture): a loose coral shirt with short hemmed sleeves, an open collar, a button placket and a chest pocket, tucked into belted cream shorts to the knee with turned-up cuffs and side seams, bare forearms and calves, and black sneakers on a paler sole. The mood is a sunny 1980s seaside holiday, but the figure is our own, never a likeness of anyone. A few chunky blocks, never smooth or lifelike, and no more than reads at the closest zoom: an octagonal head bevelled to the crown with little block ears, a mop of hair swept up into a quiff over a fringe of three locks, square eyes with a glint, brows, a wedge of a nose and a small mouth; mitten hands with a thumb, a neck, a boxy shirt bloused over a brown belt with a brass buckle, broad at the shoulders and sloping to a collar that stands round the neck, a V of skin at the collar between two flat lapels, and sneakers that spring up at the toe, their sole's edge a shade lighter than the upper so the feet read on dark cobbles. Outlined in ink round every part but the small ones (the face, ears and thumbs, collar, belt, placket, buttons, pocket, seams), and toon-shaded like the city, with knees, elbows and ankles so they can run and keep their feet flat.
- **One avatar, there from the first frame.** It stands on Grote Markt when the map opens: the map is the camera far from the avatar, not a place without one. Under 120 m of view it is its figure; further out an arrow over its head marks it (below).
- **Tap nearby to go there.** A click or tap on the ground (not a drag, a pinch or a long press) picks the walkable spot nearest it, within 8 m. Within 250 m of the avatar, it walks there along a route, or rides there on a bike. A spot it can't go to says so: a greyed marker drops there, shakes its head and goes, with a low pair of notes and a hint, "Too far to walk. Get closer first." past 250 m, "Can’t go there. Pick a street or square." where there is no ground to stand on. Nothing puts the avatar anywhere at once but a building's door ("Rooms", below).
- **Walk or run.** W A S D walk by the screen: up is away from the camera, whichever way the map is turned. Shift (or the **Run** toggle) runs at 9.2 m/s against a walk's 5.4, a sprint 1.7 times the jog, as open-world games pace it, and slower than a bike, so the bike is how to go fast. The figure turns on the spot or in an arc, never skids, and leans into curves.
- **Ride any parked bike, leave it anywhere.** F (or the **Ride** pill, which shows within 2 m of a bike) gets on the nearest bike, walking to it first if it is within 30 m. A bike handles exactly as walking does, with the same starts, stops and turns; only its look, its speed and its pedalling differ: a cruise of 11.5 m/s (about 41 km/h) and a sprint of 17.5 (63). Faster walking, with a bell: the same keys and taps steer it, and a tap within 250 m rides there. It is drawn as the traffic's own cyclist, the same figure on the same bike pedalling in the shader, in the colour it had in the rack, leaning gently into turns (a share of what a real bike would, never past 16°), with an x-ray silhouette when something hides it (for now still the old checkerboard in the menu's blue). Getting on or off takes 0.6 s. F again (or **Get off**) leaves the bike standing on its kickstand where you got off, with a clack, and the avatar steps off beside it. Up to four bikes stay where they were left; leave a fifth and the oldest goes back to its rack while nobody is looking. They aren't remembered across page loads.
- **Walkable** means anywhere a person could stroll: streets, squares, paths, lawns, car parks and open ground, over every bridge, and inside a room its floors and stairs. Never into a building (a door takes you in, "Rooms" below), nor into courtyards, tanks, the water, or through a bridge's parapet, a tree trunk, a bench, a lamp post, a café table or a bay tree by a door.
- **A house may hide you; your silhouette shows through.** The view stays at the true isometric 35.26° at every zoom, and nothing in front of the avatar is cut away. Behind a house the figure shows as a soft, flat silhouette in its shirt's coral at 45% opacity, drawn only where something nearer hides it, so an open street shows nothing and the houses themselves never change (as Diablo IV draws its hero). A steeper camera was looked at and turned down: at 65° a 7 m street is still 64% hidden, and the facades keep only 42% of their height.
- **You are never lost.** Whenever the view is too far out for the figure (over 120 m tall), a coral arrow outlined in ink points down at the avatar from over its head, bobbing gently. With the avatar off screen, the arrow sits at the edge of the screen on an inked paper disc, inside the room the on-screen controls leave, pointing toward it; tapping it ("Back to you") pins the camera to the avatar again.
- **A picked destination** gets ink-ringed pale dots along the way and a square target, Street View style.

### Rooms

- **Some doors are open.** Sint-Pieterskerk has an inside. Its door is on Grote Markt's side, in the south transept, marked on the street by a dark pointed arch. On foot within 5 m of the church, from any side, a **Go in** pill shows; it (or Enter) puts you inside, 3 m in from the door, in the same world space as the street: no second scene and no loading screen. Inside, the pill says **Go out** and puts you 2 m in front of the door. Walking out through the door leaves too; walking in through it doesn't, so the door is the one way in.
- **The building opens round you.** Its roof, towers and other parts lift away and its walls are cut down to about 9 m, lined inside, so from 35° up you look into the nave: a bluestone floor, limestone walls and pillars, wooden pews, an altar with candles, a pulpit, a font, and at the west end a stair up to the organ gallery (a wood floor, the organ, chairs and a balustrade), rising through a hole in the gallery's floor.
- **One floor at a time.** Floors above the avatar are hidden, so no ceiling is ever drawn; the floor below shows. Halfway up the stair you change floor, and the feet follow its ramp. Inside, the camera keeps between zoom 8 and 20, so the room fills the view, and the street plate names the room and floor ("Sint-Pieterskerk · Nave").
- **One look.** Interiors take the facade palette, the city's light and its ink; nothing indoors has a style of its own. The rooms are drawn by hand in `public/data/rooms.json` (docs/DATA.md, "Rooms").
- **Found from the menu too.** Places lists every building you can go into by name ("Sint-Pieterskerk", "Its door is open: walk in"), and choosing one takes the view to its door; you walk there yourself. Beside it or inside, the menu's You group offers "Go into Sint-Pieterskerk" or "Leave Sint-Pieterskerk" (↵). If the rooms can't load, the door stays shut and the hint says "The insides of the buildings couldn’t load, so their doors stay shut for now."

### Plates on the facades

- **The city names itself, the way Leuven does.** Every house OSM gives a number carries it on a small enamel plate on its front, over its door where OSM maps the door, and every street a long plate on the corner buildings where it meets another, on both sides of it, so the name reads coming up the crossing street from either way (and along any 150 m without a corner), as Belgian streets do. Numbers that would touch move apart along the wall rather than overlap. They are part of the city: lit by the sun, in the shade with their wall, hidden behind the houses in front. No labels float over the view.
- **Enamel in the app's colours.** The street plate's `--sign` blue under a `--paper` rim, paper letters (7.6:1), and a thin ink edge so a plate reads on a pale plaster wall as well as on dark brick. Rounded corners and ink sides a few centimetres deep, so from an angle it is a plate, not a sticker. House numbers are bold (800) and fill their plate; street names take the on-screen plate's letters (750), spaced a little wider than on screen so they stay apart from across the street, on a plate as long as the name.
- **Half again larger than life,** a little more than the avatar and the street furniture: a number 30 cm tall, a street plate 45 cm tall, so they read across the street at the closest zoom, on a phone too.
- **On the plaster, never on the glass.** They hang in the band the windows leave bare between the ground floor and the first: a number just over the doors and windows (over the awning of a shop), the way many Leuven numbers sit on the lintel, and a street plate above it, both under the eaves of a low house. The heights follow each house's own storeys, from its foot, as its windows do.
- **Flat on the wall.** Each plate takes the facing of the wall as it is drawn and stands a few centimetres off it, its inked sides reaching back into the wall, so none floats, sinks in or z-fights, on a slope or under a hipped roof whose walls stand on a simplified outline.
- **Only close to the street, only nearby:** when the view is under 60 m tall, numbers within 40 m and street plates within 60 m, round where the view looks. Further out they would be specks, so they don't show, and as part of the scene they don't move, so there is nothing to reduce for reduced motion and nothing for screen readers (the street sign names the street).

### Name boards on the shop fronts

- **Every shop says its name, the way a Leuven shopping street does.** Close to the street, each shop front carries its name in painted paper letters: the brand a chain's fascia shows, else the shop's own name, as OpenStreetMap has them. Like the plates, they are part of the city: lit by the sun, in the shade with their wall, hidden behind the houses in front.
- **On the awning, or over the window.** Where a front has an awning, the board is its valance: just in front of it, 1 m out from the wall and a little deeper than it, so the name reads from across the street and from down it. A pharmacy, a bank or a counter without an awning, or a front too near a corner for one, has a fascia board on the wall instead, in the band the facade paints over its shop window: above the glass, below the house number and the first floor's sills.
- **Its trade's colour, deepened.** The board is the darker of its kind's canvas and stripe (`FRONTS`), deepened by lightness alone until paper letters on it reach 4.5:1, so the hue stays (never mixed with ink, as with `--sign`): a café's green, a bar's oxblood, a frituur's red, a bakery's brown, a pharmacy's green. Paper letters (700) under a thin paper line inside an ink edge, the street plate's rim, with inked sides a few centimetres deep.
- **As wide as its front.** A board spans its awning, 10 cm short of either end, and is 0.4 m tall on an awning and 0.5 m on a fascia, the plates' half again larger than life. A long name makes it shorter, down to 0.26 and 0.32 m; past that it is cut with an ellipsis. Names longer than 22 characters are cut at a word where they can (`Brasserie De Vrijheid…`), and only Latin letters, figures and the punctuation names use are shown: a name in another script shows what it has in Latin letters, or no board.
- **Only close to the street, only nearby:** under 60 m of view, boards within 50 m, with the plates and in the same draw call.

### Railways

- **Tracks read at every zoom, detail arrives as you zoom in.** Far out, a track is a light band (running lines) or a brown one (yards). As pixels get smaller than 0.9 m the catenary masts appear, under 0.5 m both rails as ink lines, and at the closest zoom the sleepers.
- **Masts only where they'd stand.** Every 60 m along electrified running lines, on the side with room, so a yard's inner tracks stay clear.
- **The station is the wave roof.** Leuven's platform roof is the most recognisable thing from above: four white vaults across the tracks, rising from the valleys to the crowns at their real LiDAR heights. Railway land is gravel, never lawn, except where OSM maps grass or scrub on it (the embankments).
- **Not walkable:** tracks, railway land and platforms. Platforms are raised to train-floor height and the avatar stays at street level; roads under rail bridges stay walkable.
- Tunnels and covered tracks aren't drawn. Bridges get a paved deck over the water and a capped parapet on each open side; the ground follows the lie of the land, smoothed so the city's own steps (quays, cuttings, embankments) are gone, so roads stay at grade and pass underneath.

## On screen, and the menu

The map and the street are the whole screen. There is no HUD: every command, key and setting lives in one menu, opened with ⌘K or Ctrl+K (also ⌘P, Ctrl+P and `/`; `?` opens it at the controls) or the menu button. What stays on screen is only what can't wait for someone to ask:

| On screen                                    | Why it earns its place                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The data credit, top end                     | The licences (ODbL, GRB) ask for a visible credit on the map. One 10 px line with a paper halo, out of thumb reach.                                                                                                                                                         |
| The menu button, bottom end                  | Touch has no keyboard, and a mouse alone wouldn't know about ⌘K: one way in for every pointer. 40 px to the eye, 44 px to the thumb, under a right thumb. With a keyboard it wears its chord (`⌘K`), so it also teaches it.                                                 |
| The street plate, top start, close in        | Where you are is the one thing you'd otherwise have to open the menu for again and again, and it's Leuven's own enamel blue: it reads as a sign, not a control.                                                                                                             |
| Ride or Get off, bottom middle, by a bike    | The one action the moment offers that the menu would hide too well: you can't search for what you don't know is there. It appears only within 2 m of a bike, or on one.                                                                                                     |
| Go in or Go out, bottom middle, by a door    | The same reasoning: within 5 m of a building you can go into, or inside it, with its key (↵).                                                                                                                                                                               |
| The arrow, over you far out or at the edge   | Where you are once the figure is too small to see or off screen, and on the edge the way back for a thumb, which has no Space key.                                                                                                                                          |
| The hint, under the top corners, when needed | What just happened or what to do next: "Can’t go there", "Too far to walk", the coaching the first time you come close and the first ride, and what a menu command did where nothing else shows it ("Sound off", "Running"). Empty, it takes no room but stays in the page. |

Everything else (Back to you, zoom, turn, North up, Mute, Run, the Bell) is in the menu, and on its keys.

### Tokens

Tokens are oklch, inline in `index.html`, so the loading screen paints with them before any stylesheet arrives (`src/app/app.css` uses the same):

| Token                        | Use                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--ink`                      | Text, borders, hard shadows. Same family as the scene outlines.                                                                          |
| `--paper`, `--paper-press`   | Surfaces, a warm cream; pressed and hovered                                                                                              |
| `--accent`, `--accent-press` | KU Leuven-ish blue (the rider's x-ray, for now): the menu's chosen row, a primary action, the loading bar                                |
| `--sign`                     | The plates: the accent's hue a shade deeper. Deeper shades are set by lightness, never mixed with ink, which would turn the blue purple. |
| `--control`, `--bar`         | 44 px targets; 48 px pills and the menu's rows                                                                                           |
| `--radius`, `--radius-inner` | 14 px plates, pills' panels and the hint; 10 px for the menu's rows, concentric with its panel                                           |
| `--radius-large`             | 20 px: the menu, 10 px rows inside 8 px of padding and a 2 px border                                                                     |
| `--lift`, `--shadow-hard`    | The 3 px hard shadow everything pressable stands on, and the 1 px one it sinks to when pressed                                           |
| `--gutter`                   | Edge spacing, 12–20 px with the screen, plus the safe-area insets                                                                        |

### Surfaces

- 2 px ink border and a **hard drop shadow** with no blur: 3 px under buttons and plates, 6 px under the menu, which floats highest. A nod to pixel UI that needs no transparency.
- **Pills** for actions, a **circle** for the menu button, 14 px corners (`--radius`) for plates and the hint, 20 px (`--radius-large`) for the menu. Inner radii are concentric (outer radius minus padding and border).
- **Enamel plates say where you are:** Leuven on the loading screen, the street you're on, the screen's version of the plates on the facades. Nothing that looks like a plate is pressable.

### Type

- System UI font, no web fonts, so nothing to download and no layout shift.
- Weights 500–800, sizes from 0.625 rem (credits) to 1.375 rem (the loading screen's plate). The menu's search is 1.125 rem, and never under 16 px on touch, so focusing it doesn't zoom the page.
- Keys are monospace keycaps, everywhere they're named.

### The menu

- **One big sheet of inked paper** over the city: centred, 40 rem wide and up to 36 rem tall at a fixed height, so the list doesn't jump while typing; the backdrop dims the city with ink at 35%. On screens under 40 rem it's a **sheet** from the bottom edge, 85% of the height, top corners rounded, clear of the home indicator.
- **Search** at the top, the list in the middle (it scrolls; the page behind doesn't), and on a keyboard a footer that says how you're getting about ("On foot" or "On a bike") and names the keys (↑ ↓ move, ↵ choose, Esc close). On touch the footer goes, rows grow to 56 px, and the search isn't focused at open, so the keyboard doesn't cover the list.
- **Rows** in groups, each with an icon in an inked square, its name, a line on what it does, and its keys at the end: every command shows its shortcut. The chosen row is filled with the accent. A toggle says its state in words (Run **On**), never by colour alone.
- **The same groups everywhere,** since there is one map and no modes: **You** (Back to you: Space; Go into Sint-Pieterskerk or Leave Sint-Pieterskerk: ↵, when beside or inside it; Ride the bike or Get off the bike: F, when a bike is near; Run, or Sprint on a bike: Shift; Ring the bell: B, when riding), **View** (Zoom in, down to the street; Zoom out, up to the whole city; Turn left and right, the map a quarter; North up, north at the top and back to you), **General** (Time of day, Let the day pass, Traffic, Mute or Unmute, Controls and keys, About and credits), **Places** and **Keys**, every key and gesture as rows of their own, so a search for "bell" or "shift" finds them.
- **Search** forgives case, accents and word order, but every word must match: a label's start ranks above a word in it, then the keys ("f" finds Ride), then what's said about it, then the label's letters in order. **Places** always lists the buildings you can go into ("Sint-Pieterskerk"); typing two letters or more adds every named street and square (OpenStreetMap's names), the best six, each "Square · look there" or "Street · look there". Choosing one takes the view there, in to at least zoom 8, and lets go of the avatar, which stays where it is.
- **Two pages:** **Controls and keys**, every key and gesture under "The map", "On foot", "On a bike" and "Anywhere", with what touch does instead; **About**, what this is and every data source with its licence. Back (or Esc) returns to the list.
- **Keys:** ↑ ↓ move (Page Up and Down by five), ↵ chooses, Esc steps back from a page and then closes, and the chord that opened it closes it. A click on the backdrop closes it too, but not a drag that only ends there. Running a command closes the menu first. However it closes, focus goes back to the map, not to the menu button, so the map's keys work at once.
- **Accessible:** a native modal `<dialog>`, which traps focus. The search is a combobox over a labelled listbox of grouped options (`aria-activedescendant`), and the number of results is announced politely.
- It loads as its own chunk while the city is built, and is in the page (closed) by the time the map is drawn, so it opens at once. Keys pressed while it is on its way go into its search, never to the map; Esc or the chord again calls it off. If it can't be fetched, the map carries on and the hint says the menu couldn't load; the next try fetches it again.

### The street plate

- **Only close in,** when the view is under 120 m tall (as the figure is), and only when the avatar is on a named square or within 20 m of a named street. Inside a room it names the room and its floor instead: "Sint-Pieterskerk · Nave". Names are OpenStreetMap's (`src/data/streets.ts`): a square wins over the streets round it, and at a corner the street already on the plate stays until another is 4 m closer, so it doesn't flicker.
- **An enamel plate in the top-start corner:** accent-blue (`--sign`) under a paper rim, paper text, 14 px, centred, two balanced lines at most (up to 17 rem wide), with the ink border and hard shadow of everything else on the map.
- It slides in from the start, and each new name slides in after it (240–300 ms ease-out); with reduced motion, names only fade.
- It's a polite live region ("On Brusselsestraat"), so screen readers hear each new street once.

### Hints and coaching

- One or two lines, in a paper panel centred under the top corners, clear of both thumbs, always in the page (empty when there's nothing to say) so screen readers hear it change. It lets taps through to the map.
- **Coming close the first time** is coached, once the view first shows the figure: "W A S D walk, hold Shift to run, click to go somewhere. Space brings the view back to you." in keycaps where there's a fine pointer, "Tap a street to walk there. Pinch to zoom; the menu holds the rest." on touch. It stays until four seconds after the first step (a tap to go somewhere counts), and isn't shown again on that device (`localStorage`; if storage is blocked, it shows each visit).
- **The first ride** is coached once the same way: "W A S D ride, Shift sprints, B rings the bell. F gets off anywhere.", on touch "Tap a street to ride there. Ride gets you off again." Everything both teach is also in the menu's Controls and in the map's description.
- **A menu command whose effect isn't on screen** says what it did for two seconds: "Sound off", "Sound on", "Running", "Walking" (on a bike, "Sprinting" and "Riding").

### Loading

Opening the game should feel like opening a box with a little town in it. The loading screen is part of `index.html`: plain HTML, inline CSS and an inline SVG, so it paints with the first bytes, before any script or stylesheet, and nothing on it moves when the app arrives.

- **A tiny Leuven** on a slab of earth, drawn like the map (the same palette, ink outlines, isometric): a sandstone tower with a slate spire, two rows of stepped gables round a square of golden cobbles, parasols, three trees, a walker, a low sun and a cloud. Its pieces rise into place one after another (400 ms each, 60 ms apart), then it floats gently while the rest arrives.
- **The name on an enamel plate**, a line on what this is, then the **progress**: a striped accent bar and the step under way, each one the app really waits on: "Fetching the map · 286 of 767 KB" (the city file, as its bytes arrive), "Unpacking the city", "Starting the 3D engine" (if its code is still on its way), "Building the streets · 16,367 buildings", then the first frame. Building has no progress of its own, so the bar heads slowly for its end while it works. Screen readers hear each step once, not each kilobyte (`src/app/loading.ts`, `intro.ts`).
- **The keys** while it loads: "Zoom down to the street to find yourself on Grote Markt. W A S D walk, a click goes somewhere, ⌘K for everything else.", with W A S D as the keyboard types them, and Ctrl K where there is no ⌘; on touch, "Pinch down to the street to find yourself on Grote Markt, and tap where to go; the menu button holds the rest." Under them one tip of five, picked at random, among them "Sint-Pieterskerk’s door is open: walk in from Grote Markt."
- **The reveal:** the words and the town leave like the menu does (opacity, 4 px of blur, 12 px up, 150 ms), and the sky lifts off the first frame (400 ms); then the screen leaves the page.
- **An error** replaces the progress on the same screen: a plain cause and a focused **Try again**. Never a blank screen. A slow link is no error: only 20 s with nothing arriving (no bytes of the city, no engine) gives up, however long a steady download takes.

### States

| State   | Treatment                                                                               |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Loading | The loading screen, above; the map takes no input until it's drawn.                     |
| Error   | On the loading screen: a plain-language cause and a focused **Try again**.              |
| Chosen  | The menu's row under the keys or the pointer: accent fill, paper text, `aria-selected`. |
| Pressed | A button sinks into its shadow (2 px down, 1 px shadow) and darkens.                    |
| Toggled | In words on its row: **On** in ink, **Off** in outline.                                 |

### Credits

- The data licences (ODbL for OpenStreetMap, GRB terms) require visible credit on the map. It's always there: "© OpenStreetMap" and "© Digitaal Vlaanderen", linked, in one 10 px line with a paper halo in the top-end corner (each a 24 px target), wrapping to two on a narrow screen. About in the menu names every source with its licence. The smoke test fails if the credit isn't visible.

### No labels on the map

Places aren't named over the city: floating tags covered the very streets they named and fought each other for room. The street plate names where you are once the view is close in, the menu finds a street by name, and the map has a text description for screen readers.

### Keyboard

- The map is one focusable widget (`role="application"`, focused on load without a ring).
- ⌘K or Ctrl+K (and ⌘P, Ctrl+P, `/`) opens the menu; `?` opens it at the controls. Every other key works without it.
- **One map of keys, no modes,** each key with one meaning: `+`/`−` zoom, Q/E turn the map a quarter (held, they keep turning, and the map settles on the next quarter view when let go), Space (or Esc) pins the view to the avatar again, R turns north up and back to the avatar, Enter goes into the building beside you or out of it. M mutes or unmutes, T steps to the next time of day, P lets the day pass or holds it, C turns the traffic on or off. A key held down is one press, however long (only `+`/`−` keep zooming and Q/E turning), so a held F or M never acts twice. The arrow keys do nothing: the map pans by dragging, and one way to walk is easier to remember than two.
- **The avatar:** W A S D walk it relative to the screen (up is away from the camera), Shift runs. F gets on the nearest bike, walking to it first if it's within 30 m, or gets off; on a bike the same keys ride, Shift sprints and B rings the bell. A click on the ground within 250 m walks there along a route, shown as a quiet trail of small dots that shrink toward a thin ring; further, it is refused.
- **Touch:** drag looks around (and lets go of the avatar), pinch zooms, a tap on a street nearby walks (or rides) there, the arrow at the edge of the screen brings the view back, and Go in takes you through a door; there is no thumb stick. Turning, Run and the rest are in the menu.
- W A S D and Q E go by where they sit on the keyboard, so on AZERTY they are Z Q S D and A E. The menu, hints and the map's description name them as the keyboard in use types them, where the browser can say (QWERTY's letters otherwise). Keys named for what they do (M, R) go by letter.
- Shortcuts only act while focus is inside the app, and not while the menu is open or on its way.

## Motion

- **Rotation:** 280 ms ease-out. Instant with `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Press feedback:** a button sinks into its hard shadow over 120 ms ease-out (the project's own press, in place of a scale).
- **The menu** (better-ui's values): it enters with opacity, 4 px of blur and 12 px of rise over 300 ms, ease-out, and leaves with opacity, 4 px of blur and 12 px up over 150 ms; the backdrop fades with it. As a sheet on a narrow screen it slides the whole way up from the bottom edge (300 ms in, 200 ms out, ease-out), since that's where it goes back to. A native `<dialog>` with `@starting-style` and discrete `display` and `overlay` transitions, so a close mid-entrance reverses. Rows respond at once: no animation on the chosen row or on hover.
- **The loading screen:** its town rises piece by piece (400 ms, 60 ms apart) and floats; its words enter 100 ms apart (opacity, 4 px of blur, 12 px of rise, 300 ms); the bar's stripes run while it waits; the reveal is the menu's exit, then the sky fades over 400 ms.
- **The street plate:** slides in from the side (240 ms), each new name after it (300 ms).
- **Reduced motion:** nothing moves, scales or blurs; the menu, the plate's names and the loading screen's pieces only fade, the town doesn't float and the arrow doesn't bob.
- **Hover:** only on devices with hover.
- **Walking and running:** a procedural cycle that blends from walk to run with speed, feet first. A foot that is down stays where it landed, flat and then rolling onto its ball, while the body moves on over it; the legs reach for the feet, and the hips ride as high as both legs allow, so a walk bobs a little. The hips dip on the side of the swinging foot, and turn against the shoulders as the arms swing, the forward arm bending at the elbow. A run sinks onto each step and springs off into a long moment with both feet in the air, pumps bent arms mostly behind the body and leans in. Setting off leans the body in with the hips ahead of the feet; stopping leans it back, and it rocks on once, the head nodding after it, before it stands. Turning on the spot, it steps from foot to foot, its head leading the way. The torso banks into turns. Standing still, the figure breathes, glances aside, and shifts its weight onto one leg and back for seven seconds, then rests.
- **The arrow** over the avatar bobs 6 px up and back over 1.4 s, ease-in-out; at the edge of the screen it holds still. With reduced motion it doesn't bob.
- **The camera** glides by half-lives, so it moves the same at any frame rate: pinned, it closes on the avatar with a 0.18 s half-life, aiming 0.35 s of its travel ahead of it, and pinned again it glides back the same way. A zoom to a place eases with a 0.12 s half-life. With `prefers-reduced-motion` the camera follows without easing or leading, and the figure doesn't fidget. The walk cycle itself stays, since the user is moving the figure.
- **Traffic and the passing day** are the two things that move by themselves. Both run on the clock, not the frame count; both stop in a hidden tab. With `prefers-reduced-motion` the streets start empty (Traffic turns them on) and the day stands still unless asked to pass.
- **Future motion:** anything new (people, weather) goes through Emil Kowalski's animation skills. It must also respect reduced motion.

## Sound

A tiny city sounds like a toy: felt and soft wood, never game-show beeps. Sounds are few, quiet, and made on the spot with Web Audio (`src/audio`), so there is nothing to download.

| Event             | Sound                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Button or command | A soft wooden knock: a short falling triangle tone, low-passed, under a 12 ms stick of noise                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Toggle (Run)      | Two felted notes a fifth apart, rising for on, falling for off                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Footsteps         | Each time a foot lands in the walk cycle: a heel thump, a triangle at ~150 Hz gliding down an octave, under a scuff of band-passed grit. Every step is a little different in pitch, level and timing, the right foot a touch lower; a run lands higher, harder and quicker |
| Through a door    | Going in or out: a low thud, a triangle gliding from 120 to 60 Hz, under a puff of grit                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Can’t go there    | Too far, or no ground there: two quiet low notes, falling                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Freewheel         | Coasting, a tiny bright click as each of six pawls passes, about 25 a second at a cruise, quieter slowly                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Bell (B)          | A "tring": two inharmonic sine partials struck twice, ringing on for half a second                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Kickstand         | A hard little clack over a low knock, getting on and off                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Walking to a spot | A quiet tick on setting off, a lighter pair of notes on arriving                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

- **On by default, never before a gesture.** The audio context is made on the first tap, click or key after sound is on, as browsers require.
- **Mute** (M, or Mute in the menu) says "Sound off" or "Sound on" in the hint, and the menu then offers the other; the choice is remembered.
- **Quiet:** a master level of 0.4 over voices of 0.03 to about 0.2, so even a running step peaks around a twelfth of full scale.
- **Silent while the tab is hidden,** and nothing plays per frame: each sound is scheduled when its event happens.
- Every voice rises over at least 3 ms and falls exponentially (no clicks), and passes through a filter: lowpass on tones for warmth, a band on noise. These follow Raphael Salaja's `create-sound` and `generating-sounds-with-ai` rules.
- No ambience loop: the city is quiet, and a loop would cost more than it gives.
- Surfaces don't change the step yet: the walk grid doesn't know cobbles from paving.
