# City data

The map loads a single file, `public/data/city.bin`, which is about 750 KB gzipped for the whole R23 ring plus Heverlee. Walk mode adds `public/data/walk.bin` (64 KB), the house numbers and street plates on the facades, downloaded the first time someone walks. Both are committed, so a fresh clone runs without the raw data.

Everything in it can be rebuilt from free, open data with one command: data + code = map. The few things that are not data are listed under [Exceptions](#exceptions).

## Rebuild it

You need [Bun](https://bun.sh) and [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/). uv fetches Python and the pinned packages itself.

| Command              | What it does                                                                                                               |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `bun run data`       | All three steps below: fetch, build, bake. About 230 MB of downloads the first time, then seconds.                         |
| `bun run data:fetch` | Downloads every source into `data/` and checks it against `data/sources.lock.json`. Skips what it already has.             |
| `bun run data:build` | `tools/build_leuven_data.py`: joins footprints, LiDAR, orthophotos and OSM into `data/derived/`.                           |
| `bun run bake`       | `scripts/bake.ts`: keeps what lies in `AREA`, projects, simplifies and writes `public/data/city.bin` and `walk.bin`.       |
| `bun run data:check` | Rebuilds from the downloads in `data/` and fails unless the result is byte for byte the `city.bin` and `walk.bin` you had. |

The lock is the truth: a file that doesn't hash to what the lock says for the same requests is fetched again, and if the fresh download still doesn't match, the fetch fails rather than rewrite the lock. `bun scripts/fetch.ts --update` accepts the new data, for when a source really changed upstream (the monthly workflow uses it, then reports `git diff data/sources.lock.json`). A changed request (a new query, a moved `OSM_DATE`) records its new download without `--update`.

## Pipeline

```
src/data/sources.ts ─▶ bun run data:fetch ─▶ data/{osm,grb,elevation,ortho}/ ─┐
   (the manifest)        (scripts/fetch.ts)    + data/sources.lock.json       │
                                                                              ▼
public/data/city.bin ◀─ bun run bake ◀─ data/derived/ ◀─ bun run data:build (tools/build_leuven_data.py, via uv)
public/data/walk.bin ◀─┘        (and data/osm/buildings.json, for the addresses)
```

1. **Fetch.** `src/data/sources.ts` declares every source: provider, licence, credit, the request, and where its file lands. Extents are not written anywhere: they are `AREA` (`src/data/area.ts`) plus 100 m, so growing the map grows every download. `scripts/fetch.ts` plans the requests with `src/data/fetch-plan.ts` and downloads them:
   - **OpenStreetMap** through Overpass, as it was at `OSM_DATE` (Overpass answers from its history), tiled, with failover across mirrors. An answer with a `remark` (timeout, out of memory) is a failure, not data. Tiles are merged, deduplicated and sorted by type and id.
   - **GRB** layers through the WFS, in boxes small enough to come back whole: a box that returns the 2,500-feature limit is split in four. Not `startIndex` paging: the server doesn't keep its sort order between requests, so pages skip and repeat features at their edges (the hand-paged download of October 2026 had lost 152 buildings that way).
   - **DHMV II** LiDAR through the WCS as GeoTIFF tiles of at most 1650 × 1000 px on the Lambert 72 grid (larger requests fail). Tiles are checked for the TIFF signature, since errors come back as XML with HTTP 200.
   - **Orthophotos** through the WMTS, z18 tiles over each rectangle of `AREA` plus 40 m, checked for the PNG signature.

   Network errors, 429 and 5xx answers are retried with backoff. Every source's files are hashed into `data/sources.lock.json`.

2. **Build.** `tools/build_leuven_data.py` joins GRB footprints (with their courtyards) with LiDAR heights (DSM − DTM, p90), surveys each roof (eave, share of pitched pixels, the axis its slopes face, orthophoto colour), finds church towers over the nave, finds tree crowns in the LiDAR canopy, measures GRB structures (tanks and silos get their LiDAR height, bridges keep their outline), smooths the DTM into the landform and samples it every 60 m (`terrain.json`), adds OSM tags and assigns a detail tier. It then runs `tools/build_road_zones.py`, which cuts the GRB road surface (WBN) along its subdivision lines (WGO: kerbs, cycle and pedestrian zones), names each face from the OSM ways running along it, and lays out lane lines, painted cycle lanes and crossings on the carriageways. Its packages are pinned in the script header and `tools/build_leuven_data.py.lock`.
3. **Bake.** `scripts/bake.ts` keeps what lies in `AREA`, projects it to local metres, simplifies it and writes the binary `LVN1` format (`src/data/format.ts`).
   - Buildings are kept whole when every corner is inside the area; ground polygons are clipped to it.
   - Footprint vertices within 0.25 m (core) or 0.5 m (fringe) of the line through their neighbours are dropped; hero footprints keep every vertex, since landmark models are fitted to them. Ground polygons lose vertices within 0.3 m, and patches of green under 25 m² go.
   - OSM areas are closed ways and multipolygon relations: a relation's outer and inner rings are joined from its member ways, open ones chained end to end, and each hole is bridged into its outer ring by a cut of no width (`bridgeHoles` in `src/data/landcover.ts`), so a ground layer stays a list of single rings and a car park keeps its grass islands.
   - House numbers and street plates are placed on the drawn footprints and written to `walk.bin` (`src/data/plates.ts`; see [Plates on the facades](#plates-on-the-facades)).
   - Railway tracks are stored as centre lines (Douglas–Peucker to 10 cm), not polygons: the gauge is standard everywhere, so the renderer derives the ballast, sleepers and both rails. Tunnels, covered tracks and anything below ground are left out. Rails are their own layer (`City.rails`), so walking can keep off them; platforms (`City.platforms`, multipolygons joined from their outer ways) are walkable.

## Reproducibility

- **Same downloads, same bytes.** The build and bake read inputs in a fixed order, round numbers before writing them, write no timestamps, and pin the Lambert 72 transformation instead of leaving the choice to PROJ. Nothing depends on how far past `AREA` the downloads reach or how they were tiled: trees are spaced exactly and roof colours are balanced over the ring. `bun run data:check` holds them to it. It is checked on one machine at a time; floating point in the Python packages may differ between platforms, which the monthly workflow (`.github/workflows/data.yml`, on Linux) shows.
- **Same requests, (nearly) same downloads.** What each source returns over time:

  | Source        | Pinned by                       | Drifts when                                                                                                    |
  | ------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | OpenStreetMap | `OSM_DATE`                      | Never, unless a mapper's edit is redacted. Move `OSM_DATE` to take new edits.                                  |
  | DHMV II LiDAR | The product (flown 2013–2015)   | Never: it is a finished survey.                                                                                |
  | GRB           | Nothing: the WFS serves today's | Digitaal Vlaanderen updates footprints and roads continuously; features in this area changed in October 2026.  |
  | Orthophotos   | Nothing: "most recent" mosaic   | A new winter flight replaces it, about once a year. The dated layers (`omwrgb24vl`, …) are a different mosaic. |

  `data/sources.lock.json` records each source's files, size and hash, so `git diff data/sources.lock.json` after a fetch into an empty `data/` tells you exactly which source moved (the monthly workflow does this). Commit the lock together with `city.bin`.

## Adding a source

New data comes through the manifest and the pipeline, never by a manual download.

1. Add an entry to `SOURCES` in `src/data/sources.ts`. Pick the kind (`overpass`, `wfs`, `wcs` or `wmts`), a kebab-case `id`, its `output` under `data/`, its provider (add one to `PROVIDERS`, with its licence and credit, if it is new) and what reads it in `usedBy`. Don't write coordinates: the request follows `AREA`.
   - Another kind of service needs a planner and a check in `src/data/fetch-plan.ts` (tested) and a case in `scripts/fetch.ts`. Prefer open formats a script can read without a GIS stack: GeoJSON, JSON, GeoTIFF, PNG.
2. List it in the Sources table above and in [data/README.md](../data/README.md). The sources test fails until this page names every id.
3. Run `bun run data:fetch` (it downloads only the new source), use it in the build or the bake, then `bun run data` and `bun run verify`.
4. Commit the code, `data/sources.lock.json`, `public/data/city.bin` and `walk.bin` together. If what you draw from it is a choice rather than data, add it to [Exceptions](#exceptions).

## What the data decides

Prefer reading the world over guessing it. Each of these replaced a rule of thumb:

| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pitched or flat roof      | OSM `roof:shape`, else the LiDAR: over 40% of the roof sloped 15–60° is pitched                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | `roofFromSurvey` in `src/data/city.ts`                                                                         |
| Roof rise and wall height | LiDAR p90 (ridge) minus p15 (eave), capped by the footprint's width                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | `planRoof` in `src/scene/buildings.ts`                                                                         |
| Roof shape                | Gable on near-rectangles, else a hipped roof over the footprint's straight skeleton                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | `src/data/skeleton.ts`                                                                                         |
| Ridge direction           | The LiDAR's slope vectors over the roof (their structure tensor): where 65% of their steepness points along the footprint's long side, the gable's ridge turns across it, as on terraced houses whose ridge runs along the street. A turned ridge whose span would pass 18 m keeps it along, and its gables, on the party walls, aren't stepped                                                                                                                                                                    | `facing` in `tools/build_leuven_data.py`, `roofAcross` in `src/data/city.ts`, `planRoof`                       |

The sign shows the named square under the walker, else the nearest named street within 20 m                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | `src/data/streets.ts`, `scripts/bake.ts`                                                                       |
| House numbers             | OSM `addr:housenumber` and `addr:street` on buildings (their middle) and on points, on the drawn footprint they lie in (or one within 3 m), on the wall that faces the named street or square                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | `placeHouseNumbers` in `src/data/plate-placement.ts`                                                           |
| Street plates             | Junctions are nodes shared by OSM ways of different names; each street gets a plate on the corner building that faces it, and one along any 150 m without one                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | `junctions`, `placeStreetPlates` in `src/data/plate-placement.ts`                                              |
| Where you can walk        | All ground on the map, minus water, railway land and platforms; road surfaces, squares, paths and bridge decks open those again; minus every building footprint (and its courtyards), tank and bridge parapet, and every drawn trunk, bench, rack, statue and parasol foot                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | `WalkGrid` in `src/data/walkable.ts`                                                                           |
| Ground height             | The DHMV II DTM, averaged into 5 m blocks, smoothed by three 75 m box passes (σ ≈ 37 m) and sampled every 60 m on a grid anchored at Grote Markt, whose ground is y = 0. Read as triangles: ground layers are cut along their edges and draped, buildings stand on the lowest ground under their corners, trees and furniture on the ground at their point                                                                                                                                                         | `tools/build_leuven_data.py`, `src/data/terrain.ts`, `src/scene/cells.ts`                                      |

Walkability is layered, with no hand-placed exceptions: dry ground is open, water, railway land and platforms close it, roads, squares, paths and bridge decks open it again, and buildings, tanks and parapets close it on top, then trunks and street furniture as they are drawn (lamp posts only for the body, not the cells, so a narrow pavement keeps its route). A walk starts on a street cell (a road, square, path or bridge), never on open ground: behind a block that can be a garden walled in by houses, with no way out. A new layer is open unless `WalkGrid` is taught to close it. Where tracks cross a road at grade, the road opens the railway land again.

| How a house number reads                           | `plateText` in `src/data/plate-placement.ts`                                                               | A building OSM gives several numbers (`80;82;84`) shows the first to the last as a range (`80–84`), as Leuven's own plates do; numbers over 8 characters are left out.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Tuning                                             | Next to each rule: `PITCHED_SHARE`, `SIMPLIFY`, `MIN_GREEN_AREA`, tree spacing…                            | Parameters of the rules in [What the data decides](#what-the-data-decides), documented where they are defined. They change how data is read, never what is placed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

No download is ever edited by hand. When a source is wrong, fix it upstream (OSM is open to edits; move `OSM_DATE` forward to pick it up) or write a rule for it.

Known limits of the data itself: the LiDAR was flown in 2013–2015, so buildings built or demolished since have wrong heights, and overhanging trees can inflate the height of small buildings (the bake caps the clearest cases, sheds standing alone under a crown).

## Kinds of ground

Every OSM area in `osm-areas` goes into at most one ground layer (`coverOf` in `src/data/landcover.ts`); where it carries several tags, the first row that matches decides. `bun scripts/audit-areas.ts` tallies every combination of land tags in the download by the area it covers inside `AREA`, with the layer it lands in, and flags the large ones that land in none.

| Layer      | OSM tags                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Drawn                                   |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| `plazas`   | `place=square`, any `area:highway` (and closed pedestrian ways from `osm-roads`)                                                                                                                                                           | Setts, above the roads; walkable, named |
| `water`    | `natural=water`, `landuse=basin\|reservoir`, `waterway=riverbank`                                                                                                                                                                          | River blue; closes walking              |
| `parking`  | `amenity=parking`, unless `parking=underground\|multi-storey\|rooftop`; `leisure=schoolyard`                                                                                                                                               | Asphalt, a shade lighter than the roads |
| `pitches`  | `leisure=pitch\|track`                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Bright mown green                       |
| `railLand` | `landuse=railway`                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Gravel; closes walking                  |
| `woods`    | `landuse=forest\|orchard`, `natural=wood\|scrub`, `landcover=trees\|scrub`                                                                                                                                                                 | Deep green, under the trees             |
| `green`    | `leisure=park\|garden\|playground\|dog_park`, `landuse=grass\|meadow\|cemetery\|recreation_ground\|village_green\|allotments\|flowerbed\|vineyard\|greenery`, `natural=grassland\|heath\|wetland`, `landcover=grass`, `amenity=grave_yard` | Lawn green                              |
| none       | Everything else: `landuse=residential\|commercial\|retail\|industrial\|construction\|farmland\|religious…`, the grounds of `amenity=school\|university\|hospital`, `leisure=sports_centre`                                                 | The slab's garden-green base            |

The layers are drawn from the most generic to the most specific, a few millimetres apart: railway land, green, woods, pitches, car parks, then water, paths, the road surface and squares. So what is mapped inside something else shows: grass on an embankment, a wood in a park, a pitch or a car park in either. School, campus and hospital grounds are left as base on purpose: they hold buildings, lawns, yards and car parks that OSM maps on their own, and one colour for all of it would be wrong over most of it. Squares stay above the road surface, so a lawn mapped on a square (about 0.7 ha in all) is under its setts.

The October 2026 audit, before and after relations were fetched (ha inside `AREA`): car parks 21.7 → 24.6 (Parking Bodart, relation 14333531, was missing and showed the base and green round it), woods 49.5 → 63.5 (`natural=wood\|scrub` and forest relations), green 276.6 → 293.1, water 20.7 → 21.9, squares 7.9 → 8.7. Land drawn as nothing: 417 → 475 ha, of which 330 ha is `landuse=residential` and 30 ha university grounds.

## The area

`AREA` is a few abutting, non-overlapping rectangles, so the diorama can follow the city instead of a single box: today the R23 ring with the station and its whole track bundle, and a band through Heverlee from Arenbergpark and its castle to the Abdij van Park. The slab's earth sides hang from the outline of their union, and the camera's target stays inside it.

## Growing the map

1. Change `AREA` in `src/data/area.ts`.
2. Run `bun run data`: every download follows `AREA`, so the fetch picks up the new ground and drops tiles that fell outside it.
3. Run `bun run verify`. The budgets tell you when the city got too heavy. Commit `city.bin` and `walk.bin` with `data/sources.lock.json`.

## Plates on the facades

`walk.bin` (`LVW1`, `src/data/plates.ts`) holds walk mode's house numbers and street plates: where each hangs (decimetres, a few centimetres proud of its wall so rounding never sinks it in), which way its wall faces (a byte), and what it says (house numbers in a table of strings; street plates as their name's place in `city.bin`'s `streetNames`, so the two files are baked and shipped together). Only a walk downloads it; the map never does.

**House numbers.** Every address OSM maps (`addr:housenumber` with `addr:street`): the buildings' first, at the middle of their outline, then the points (shops, offices) whose address no building has. Each hangs on the drawn GRB footprint it lies in, or the nearest within 3 m, where its own point meets the wall (so a block with several addresses spreads them along its front, half a metre from the corners). The wall is, in order:

1. one that faces its square, when the address is on a named square;
2. else the nearest wall that faces its street's centre line (its normal within 60° of the way there, at most 40 m off) and sees it past every other building;
3. else, for a house down a lane or in a court, the wall nearest its street that opens onto a road or square.

An address on the same street and number as one already placed (a shop inside a numbered building) hangs once, and one within 45 cm of another plate is left out. The bake logs the counts: of 12,979 addresses in the area, 11,427 are placed (10,876 facing their street, 256 their square, 295 onto public ground); 797 duplicates, 518 with no wall that faces their street, 164 with no footprint near, 58 crowded, 10 on landmarks (their models stand proud of the footprint) and 5 unreadable are left out.

**Street plates.** A junction is a node shared by OSM ways of different names, before any simplifying; nodes within 20 m are one junction, so a dual carriageway or a small roundabout counts once. For each street there, a plate hangs on the nearest wall within 25 m that faces that street squarely (its normal within 45°, the street at most 20 m off and in sight), at the point nearest the junction that keeps the plate clear of the corner, unless a plate of that street already hangs within 30 m. Then each street is walked in 25 m steps, and wherever no plate of its name hangs within 150 m, one goes on the nearest wall that faces it. A plate is wide enough for its name, so a wall too short for it is passed over. The bake logs 1,154 plates at junctions and 134 along stretches, 1,288 in all; 315 times a street found no corner wall at a junction (open squares, parks, bridges).

## Format changes

Bump `VERSION` in `format.ts` (or in `plates.ts` for `walk.bin`) and the cache name in `public/sw.js` together. The round-trip tests in `format.test.ts` and `plates.test.ts`, and `baked.test.ts`, guard the rest.
